# Medicare Advantage in 11 Septembers: How Plans Grew Before AEP 2027

*Annual Enrollment starts October 15. Before the new plan year, here is a look back at how the market actually moved, using CMS's own enrollment files.*

![National MA enrollment, 2015 to 2026](national_ma_members_2015-2026.png)

Every fall, the conversation about Medicare Advantage turns to what is new for next year. Before looking ahead, I wanted to look back. I pulled the CMS Monthly Enrollment by Contract files for every September from 2015 through 2026 and built a profile of every parent organization in every state: how many contracts, how many plan benefit packages (PBPs), how many members, and how many counties it covers.

Then I put it in an interactive dashboard so you can run your own state. The link is at the end. Here is what stood out.

## 1. Enrollment more than doubled

Counting H-contract plans and excluding retiree and employer-group plans, September enrollment went from **13.0 million in 2015 to 30.0 million in 2026**, about 2.3 times as many members. Growth was steady every year, with the fastest stretch from 2019 to 2022, when enrollment rose by about 7 million.

## 2. Growth came with a lot of plan choice, then a trim

The number of contracts in the data climbed from 720 in 2015 to a peak of 934 in 2024, and has since eased to 882. The count of plan-by-state offerings followed the same arc: about 19,500 in 2015, a peak near 37,300 in 2022, and about 36,300 today. In other words, the market added choice for most of the decade and has recently started to consolidate it.

## 3. The largest parents got larger

The three largest parent organizations held **40.6%** of enrollment in 2015 and hold **55.6%** in 2026. The top five went from 51.1% to 65.5%.

![Share by parent organization](national_parent_share_2015-2026.png)

A few movements worth noting:

- **UnitedHealth Group** rose from 16.8% to 25.2% (with a high of 28.1% in 2025).
- **Humana** moved from 17.2% to 21.1%, and was the largest parent in 2015 by a narrow margin.
- **CVS Health (Aetna)** went from 5.5% to 9.3%.
- **Kaiser Permanente** slipped from 6.5% to 4.9% while still growing in absolute members, since the overall market grew faster.
- **Centene** (with Health Net and WellCare) moved from 5.0% to 3.0%.
- **Devoted Health** did not appear in 2015 and has about 520,000 members in 2026, or 1.7%.

Parents are grouped by corporate lineage in this analysis (for example, Aetna into CVS, WellCare and Health Net into Centene, HealthSpring into Cigna and later HCSC). That grouping is my own and ownership changes can move enrollment between parents from one year to the next.

## 4. Every state grew, at very different speeds

![State growth multiples](state_growth_multiple_2015-2026.png)

Among the largest states, Georgia grew about 3.9 times, Illinois 3.1 times, Michigan and North Carolina about 3.0 times, and Texas 2.7 times. California, the largest market at roughly 3.0 million members in 2026, grew about 1.65 times, which reflects how early and how deeply it was already penetrated. Nevada grew about 2.2 times.

## 5. A closer look at three states

California, North Carolina and Nevada are the three states the dashboard puts first, because they show three different market shapes: a mature, concentrated market (California), a fast-growing one with many entrants (North Carolina), and a smaller market where one ownership change can move the rankings (Nevada). Smaller, regional plans show up clearly in these views: Alignment Health, for example, went from about 19,600 members in one state in 2015 to about 296,000 in five states in 2026, reaching 7.9% of California enrollment.

## How to read the numbers

- **Plans** are H-contracts. **Products** are PBPs with a service area in the state. Average members per product is members divided by PBPs.
- **County coverage** is the share of the state's counties where an organization has a plan with reported enrollment. **Member reach** is the share of the state's MA members living in those counties. Both are lower bounds of the true service area.
- CMS suppresses county enrollment of 10 or fewer, which I count as 0. Regional PPOs (R-contracts) and PACE are not counted.
- Everything is based on CMS reported membership count, taken each September, so it reflects the plan year in progress, not the following one.
